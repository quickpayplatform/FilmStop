How to compare video production proposals

What to look for when two quotes describe the same project but come in at different prices.

November 12, 2026 · Film Stop

Request a production quote

Write down what you get at delivery

Put the final assets side by side. One quote might include a two-minute film and three social edits. Another might list “social deliverables” without saying how many or whether they’re edited separately. Get the quantities, lengths, and formats in writing.

Look at the assumptions behind shoot day

Ask which locations and hours the estimate covers, how many crew members are expected, and who provides talent, props, or access. If interviews are in two offices, ask whether travel and setup are included. The assumptions explain a lot of the difference.

Read the editing section carefully

Check whether captions, color, audio cleanup, music, and graphics are included. Ask when revision rounds begin and whether your team needs to send one set of notes. Agree on how added versions or changes to approved material will be quoted.

Ask for one alternate scope

If a proposal is over budget, ask the producer to show what they’d adjust. One location with a strong interview and planned supporting shots might get you to the same goal. A cheaper package makes sense when you can still see how it serves the brief.
