How to choose a video production company in Baltimore

What to look for before you book a crew, from finished work to how the team handles changes.

November 12, 2026 · Film Stop

Talk with Film Stop

Watch a whole video

A reel can tell you whether you like the look. Ask for a finished project that’s close to yours and watch it all the way through. Listen to how the interviews sound. Notice whether you understand the point of the film by the end. Ask what the crew actually handled on that job.

Ask who will be on the job

Find out who runs the shoot, who edits, and who will answer your questions after filming. If you need to coordinate executives, security or a busy venue, ask how the producer would schedule that day. Give them one real complication and see what they come back with.

Compare the same deliverables

Send each team the same brief. Ask for the number of shoot days, the length and format of each edit, the revision rounds, and the delivery date. A proposal that includes three finished cuts is a different scope from one that includes a single master.

Check the location and your own calendar

A local crew can still need time to load in, find a quiet room, and get approval to film. Share what you know about parking, room availability, and access. Pick the team that can explain how those details affect your day and your budget.

Talk through your project

Send us your goal, a reference you like, and your deadline. We can share relevant work and talk through what your shoot would take.
