Hiring a crew

How to choose a Baltimore video production company

Compare studios on the work you need, how they run a project, and what their proposal actually includes.

November 12, 2026 · Film Stop

Ask to see complete work

A reel shows range in a minute. A finished piece tells you how the crew structures an interview, handles sound, and earns the ending. Ask which part of the piece the company owned and what constraints shaped it.

Listen for the producer’s questions

A producer should be curious about who needs to see the video, where it will live, what access you have, and who can approve it. Give two studios the same brief so you can compare how they solve the same problem.

Compare what happens after the shoot

Find out who edits, who manages feedback, and which deliverables are included. Ask how the team handles music, caption files, archiving, and requests for raw footage. Get those answers into the proposal if they affect your project.

Talk through one realistic complication

Mention a tight venue window, an executive who may only have twenty minutes, or a deadline tied to a launch. Ask how the producer would schedule around it. You will learn more about how the crew works than you would from a gear list.
