Corporate video production

Corporate video production, from brief to final cut

A practical look at how a corporate video comes together and what your team should prepare.

November 12, 2026 · Film Stop

Start a corporate video brief

Start with one audience

A recruiting film, a customer story, and an internal launch can all feature the same office, but each has a different job. Write down who’s watching and what you want them to understand or do. That sentence will help when the team has to choose between two good ideas.

Find something the camera can show

If the message is that your team makes onboarding easy, show a real handoff or walk through what a customer sees on day one. Give interviewees prompts ahead of time so they can think of specific examples. Leave space for them to talk in their own words.

Make the shoot easy for your staff

Book a quiet room, let the crew know how to get in, and tell each participant how much time to hold. Identify any screens, badges, customer information, or areas that should stay out of frame. Share a contact who can handle questions during the shoot.

Review the story before the polish

On the first cut, check the message, accuracy, and pacing. Send consolidated notes with timestamps. Once the structure is approved, review names, graphics, captions, and the final files in the place they’ll actually be shown.

Plan your corporate video

Share who the video needs to reach, where it will run, and when you need it.
