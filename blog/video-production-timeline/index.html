How to build a realistic video production timeline

Work backward from launch and reserve time for access, approvals, revisions, and delivery.

November 12, 2026 · Film Stop

Separate the launch date from delivery

A conference film may need to reach the venue several days before playback. A paid campaign may need time for uploads and internal review. Ask whoever publishes the video when they need the final files, then use that date to schedule the edit.

Put approvals on the calendar

Reserve dates for treatment, script, rough cut, and final approval. Name the person who can collect feedback when several people review. Ask reviewers to flag factual, brand, or compliance concerns on the script or rough cut, while those changes are easier to make.

Treat access as part of scheduling

Check when the location is quiet, when the people on camera are available, and how long load-in takes. If the project relies on weather or an exterior setup, discuss what the crew can film if conditions change.

Reduce scope when a date is fixed

For a tight launch, consider one location, fewer approval steps with the same decision-maker, or a smaller launch package followed by additional versions. Have the producer confirm what can fit before telling stakeholders the date is locked.
