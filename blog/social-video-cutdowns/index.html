How to plan social cutdowns from one video shoot

Turn a production day into several purposeful edits by planning their openings, framing, and calls to action in advance.

November 12, 2026 · Film Stop

Write the short versions first

Give each cut one idea. A quick demo might answer how a product works, while a customer clip might answer why someone switched. Write an opening shot or spoken line for each cut so the crew knows what must happen on set.

Check framing before the talent moves on

A centered close-up can often crop well, but two people on opposite sides of a wide frame may disappear in a vertical edit. Review framing on set and capture a dedicated vertical take if the action needs it. Leave room for captions and interface elements.

Record clean pieces the editor can move

Capture standalone answers, clear product inserts, and enough room tone for clean edits. Record a few endings with different calls to action when the same message will be used in different placements. An editor can assemble a more coherent short when each piece has a clear beginning and end.

Create a simple delivery matrix

For each edit, list audience, platform, duration, aspect ratio, caption format, and CTA. Give reviewers the list so they can tell whether the vertical demo and the wide homepage cut are doing their intended jobs.

Scope a shoot around multiple deliverables

Share the platforms you use and what you need each video to accomplish. We can build the shot list around the final edits.
